What it holds, and what it cannot

check_circleFour facts. The code, the equipment with its count, the site, and the period. At 62px a headline is two lines at most, and a fifth row would shrink all of them.

blockNot the three terms. Fuel, mob & demob and payment do not fit here — so they live in the HTML card below, and the image never contradicts them by showing two of the three.

blockNothing clickable. The orange pill is a picture of a button. It works because the whole card is the link — but it must never be the only place an instruction appears.

translateArabic mirrors, numbers do not. The layout flips; the code, the dates and the count stay left-to-right.

The three terms, and why these three

drawA real renter typed exactly these by hand in an email on 18 Aug 2026: “DIESEL Is Sajco Scope · Mob by & demob By Supplier · Duration : 1Month & extendable”. Duration rides with the dates above, because it is the same fact.

ruleA row appears only when the request carries it. Never “Payment: —”. An empty row teaches a supplier to skim the block, and then he skims the row that mattered.

visibility_offEverything else is left out on purpose. Hours, operator, nationality, certificates, night shift, overtime, SLA — the bid form asks each one structurally and gets an answer back as data instead of prose.

3 · What has to be true in every client

sync_altThe two never disagree. Whatever the image cannot hold must appear in the card — and nothing in the card may contradict the image.

mailGmail never unfurls a pasted URL. By design: it will not fetch the page. The only way a card reaches Gmail is on the clipboard as HTML, which copyBidLink already writes.

styleInline styles only. A <style> block is stripped by most mail clients, and flex and grid are ignored by Outlook — which is why the card is a table.

link_offThe image is a fallback, not the message. A recipient with images turned off must still get the request from the text.
